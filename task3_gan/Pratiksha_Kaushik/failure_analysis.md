# Failure Analysis: CycleGAN Monet ↔ Photo (final model, v3)

Model: average of the EMA generators at epochs 60, 65 and 70 (λ_cyc 10, λ_id 1, 2-scale PatchGAN), with colour calibration on Monet→photo. All examples come from the notebook's error-analysis section (§12) and are generated by this model. Numbers like `#21` are indices into the 1,000 held-out photos (photo→Monet) or the 300 Monets (Monet→photo).

**How failures were found:** cases were ranked automatically by two signals, then inspected by eye:

- **lowest content cosine:** Inception features of the input vs the translation. The scene structure changed the most.
- **highest reconstruction LPIPS:** input vs photo→Monet→photo. The cycle failed to come back.

## 1. Night and low-light scenes are washed out (photo→Monet)

| Example | What happens | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| #21: night coastline with city lights | Black sky and land become a hazy blue-grey; the lights turn into faint smudges | Monet's 300 paintings contain almost no true night scenes, so the generator maps "dark" onto his typical bright, pastel palette. |
| #585: city skyline at night | Dark scene lifted to a murky green-grey; skyscraper lights smeared into horizontal streaks | Same cause: no target-domain examples of deep blacks with point lights, so the generator has nothing to imitate and averages towards mid-tones. |

The lifted shadows are partly *correct* Monet style (he rarely used pure black). But here the scene's meaning (night) is lost, which is why these photos have the lowest content similarity.

## 2. Sky-only scenes get blotchy textures and horizontal streaks (photo→Monet)

| Example | What happens | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| #197: dramatic sunset clouds | Cloud shapes survive, but the sky fills with mottled patches and a faint horizontal band near the horizon | Large smooth regions give the generator little structure to follow, so the PatchGAN-driven texture is applied everywhere, including where the photo was smooth. |
| #150: sunset over water with boats | Colours wash out to pale beige; a brushy, noisy texture covers the whole sky | With almost no edges, the "Monet texture" dominates the content. |
| #579: pastel lake at dusk | Horizontal streaking along the horizon and water | Repeated horizontal bands are a typical transposed-convolution / PatchGAN artefact in low-detail regions. |

## 3. Dense fine texture is destroyed (photo→Monet, worst cycle reconstruction)

| Example | What happens | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| #594: deer in tall golden grass | Grass becomes a pale, blotchy wash; the deer turns into a dark blue silhouette; the reconstruction gains a bluish patch | Thousands of thin grass blades can't survive being repainted as broad strokes, so the information is gone and the cycle can't restore it. |
| #313: pond covered in leaves | Leaves blur into a smeared mosaic; the reconstruction is darker and loses the leaf detail | High-frequency detail is the first thing replaced by stroke texture. |
| #597: reeds over blue water | Green/brown reeds turn orange-yellow, and the error persists in the reconstruction | The colour change is large enough that the reverse generator treats it as content, not style, and keeps it. |
| #852, #714: forests with many thin trunks and leaves | Translation looks plausible, but the reconstruction is softer, with lost branch and leaf detail | Same mechanism; these rank high on reconstruction LPIPS rather than being visually bad translations. |

These are the main reason the photo-cycle reconstruction (L1 0.117) is worse than the Monet cycle (0.073): photos have far more fine detail than paintings.

## 4. Saturated colours are drained (photo→Monet)

| Example | What happens | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| #20: tree in a bright yellow rapeseed field | The vivid yellow becomes a pale, washed-out yellow-green; the sky goes grey-violet | Monet's palette is softer and more muted than modern saturated photography, so the generator compresses strong colours towards it. |
| #597: reeds (see above) | Natural greens shift towards orange | Over-applied palette shift on a colour that's rare in the Monet set. |

The tree's shape survives well, so this counts as a style over-correction rather than a structural failure.

## 5. Foggy, low-contrast Monets become hallucinated scenes (Monet→photo)

The most severe failures in either direction.

| Example | What happens | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| #75: *Charing Cross Bridge* in fog | The pale blue-white painting becomes a golden-orange sunset; the bridge is no longer visible in the "photo" | There's no realistic photo equivalent of Monet's fog, so the generator invents a plausible photo scene with strong light and ignores the faint structure. |
| #291: *Waterloo Bridge* in violet haze | Turned into a saturated cyan-and-white sky; the bridge arches dissolve | The input is almost uniform pastel colour, so the generator fills it with a generic bright-sky look. |
| #185: river scene in thick fog | Becomes a dark night scene with a bright cyan blob artefact | The low-information input pushes the generator into an unrealistic region of its output space; the cyan blob is a classic GAN artefact. |

Monet→photo is the harder direction: the generator has to *add* detail a painting never contained. Foggy series paintings (London bridges) are the worst case because they contain the least structure. This matches the metrics: Monet→photo recall is only 0.44, so outputs cover a narrow part of real photo variety.

## 6. Brush-stroke texture leaks into "photos" (Monet→photo)

| Example | What happens | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| #215: village with a church above an icy river | Colours look more photographic (darker, with a pink glow), but the ice and buildings keep visible paint strokes | The generator changes colour and contrast more easily than texture; removing strokes would require inventing real surface detail. |
| #140: rocky coastline | Sky and sea become more photo-like, while the rocks keep their painted texture | Same cause: texture is the hardest part of "painting → photo" to undo. |

## 7. Hidden information: why good cycle L1 doesn't prove good translations

Several failures show the same pattern: content disappears from the translation but **reappears almost perfectly** in the reconstruction.

| Example | Translation | Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| #75 (Monet→photo) | Bridge not visible (golden sunset) | Bridge back, pastel fog restored |
| #21, #585 (photo→Monet) | Night scenes brightened to grey-blue haze | Darkness and city lights restored |
| #20 (photo→Monet) | Yellow field drained to pale yellow | Vivid yellow restored |

This is the CycleGAN "steganography" effect (Chu et al., 2017): the generators learn to hide information about the input in small, nearly invisible signals in the output, so the reverse generator can undo the translation. It explains why the cycle-reconstruction errors are so low (0.117 and 0.073, against 0.574 for unrelated images) even for visibly poor translations. Low cycle L1 shows the implementation works, not that each translation is faithful.

## What works (for contrast)

From `figures/qual_photo2monet.png`, the model does well on:

- **Coastlines, waves, waterfalls and water reflections:** pastel palette, softened edges and brush-like texture, with the scene layout fully preserved.
- **Mid-tone landscapes with clear structure:** cliffs, shorelines and skylines with good contrast.

These are close to the kinds of scenes Monet painted most (water, coast, light on surfaces), which is why they work. Even good outputs show lifted shadows and slightly hazy blacks. That's consistent with Monet's style, but it's the same mechanism that breaks night scenes (category 1).

## Patterns and root causes

1. **Training-data coverage.** Failures cluster on content the 300 Monets don't cover: night, deep blacks, saturated modern colours, dense fine texture. Where Monet painted a lot (water, coasts, light), results are good.
2. **Little structure means more hallucination.** Smooth skies (category 2) and foggy paintings (category 5) give the generator nothing to anchor to, so texture and invented content take over.
3. **Global brightness and colour aren't the main driver.** Content similarity barely correlates with input brightness (r = +0.14) or colourfulness (r = −0.02). Failures depend on *what* is in the scene, not on its average colour.
4. **The cycle constraint can be satisfied by hiding information** (category 7), so it doesn't fully prevent content changes.

## Possible fixes (not tried, time budget)

| Failure | Possible fix |
|---|---|
| Night scenes, saturated colours | More varied Monet data (not allowed here), or a weaker style push for very dark inputs |
| Streaks and blotches in smooth regions | Resize-convolution instead of transposed convolution in the generator; stronger discriminator regularisation (spectral norm) |
| Lost fine texture | Higher-capacity generator, or a perceptual/feature cycle loss instead of pure pixel L1 |
| Fog hallucination (Monet→photo) | Stronger cycle or identity weight for the M2P direction only; more training on foggy-style inputs |
| Hidden information | Add noise or JPEG-style degradation to translations before the reverse generator, so hidden signals can't survive |
